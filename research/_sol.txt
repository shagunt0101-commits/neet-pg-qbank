195. According to Park’s classification, which type of 
anorectal fistula is depicted in the image shown?
(A) Intersphincteric
(B) Suprasphincteric
(C) Extrasphincteric
(D) High transsphincteric
Ans :
(A) Intersphincteric
Park's classification is the most widely used system for categorizing anorectal fistulas based on their relationship with the anal sphincter muscles. The image clearly depicts a fistula tract that originates from the dentate line, travels downwards within the plane between the internal anal sphincter and the external anal sphincter, and exits onto the perianal skin. This path is the definition of an intersphincteric fistula. This is the most common type of anorectal fistula, accounting for approximately 45% of all cases as indicated in the diagram. A transsphincteric fistula would cross both sphincters, a suprasphincteric fistula would loop up and over the puborectalis muscle, and an extrasphincteric fistula would bypass the sphincter complex entirely, connecting the rectum to the skin.

The image highlights the jugular foramen, a 
large, irregular opening at the base of the skull, 
located in the posterior cranial fossa between 
the petrous part of the temporal bone and the 
occipital bone. This foramen is a crucial conduit 
for several vital structures. It transmits three 
cranial nerves : the glossopharyngeal nerve 
(CN IX), the vagus nerve (CN X), and the 
accessory nerve (CN XI). It also allows passage 
for the sigmoid sinus as it becomes the internal 
jugular vein. The hypoglossal nerve (CN XII), 
in contrast, does not pass through the jugular 
foramen. Instead, it exits the skull through its 
own dedicated canal, the hypoglossal canal, 
which is located anterosuperior to the occipital 
condyle. Therefore, the hypoglossal nerve is the 
structure listed that does not traverse the jugular 
foramen.
