(B) Tangier disease
The combination of pathognomonic clinical and laboratory findings is diagnostic of Tangier disease. This is a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by a severe deficiency or absence of high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol in the blood. The underlying genetic defect is in the ABCA1 gene, which codes for a transporter that is essential for the efflux of cholesterol from peripheral cells to form HDL particles. The hallmark laboratory finding is extremely low HDL levels (often <5 mg/dL) with normal or low LDL and moderately elevated triglycerides. The clinical hallmark is the deposition of cholesteryl esters in the reticuloendothelial system, which causes enlarged, orange-yellow colored tonsils, as well as hepatosplenomegaly and a peripheral neuropathy.
