Hexokinase and pyruvate kinase are key regulatory enzymes of glycolysis, not gluconeogenesis. === q2726 | Surgery / Hepatobiliary Surgery ===
Q: A 40-year-old female undergoes cholecystectomy and later develops postoperative abdominal pain. Which test is used to detect a bile leak?
A. ERCP
B. MRCP
C. HIDA
D. CECT
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: When a post-cholecystectomy bile leak is suspected, the most sensitive and specific functional imaging study to identify the leak is a HIDA scan (Hepatobiliary Iminodiacetic Acid scan), also known as cholescintigraphy. This is a nuclear medicine test where a radioactive tracer (e.g., technetium-99m labeled mebrofenin) is injected intravenously. The tracer is taken up by the hepatocytes and excreted into the biliary system, mimicking the flow of bile. The flow of the tracer is imaged over time with a gamma camera. In a patient with a bile leak, the scan will show the tracer flowing normally through the bile ducts but will also demonstrate the abnormal extravasation of the tracer from the biliary tree into the peritoneal cavity, confirming the presence, and often the location, of the leak. While MRCP shows anatomy and ERCP is therapeutic, HIDA is the best test to functionally demonstrate an active leak.
