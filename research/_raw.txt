Which mosquito species lacks a siphon tube and \nrests parallel to the surface of water?\n(A) Anopheles\n(B) Culex\n(C) Aedes\n(D) Mansonia\nAns :\n(A) Anopheles\nThe larval stage of mosquitoes is aquatic, and different genera can be identified by their distinct morphology and resting posture in the water. The larvae of the Anopheles genus are unique in that they lack a respiratory siphon. Instead, they have palmate hairs on their abdominal segments that allow them to lie flat and rest parallel to the water surface to breathe through spiracular openings. In contrast, the larvae of Culex (B) and Aedes (C) both possess a respiratory siphon tube, and they hang down from the water surface at an angle to breathe through this tube. Mansonia (D) larvae have a specialized, pointed siphon that they use to pierce the roots of aquatic plants to obtain oxygen, so they are not found at the surface. The combination of no siphon and a parallel resting position is the key identifying feature of anopheline larvae.\n34.
